Sir, Have Some Fucking Dignity.

On male entitlement, medical authority, and the extraordinary indignity of being told no by a woman.

November 12, 2026

The Soundtrack

His eyes widened. His face turned crimson. The veins in his forehead bulged as he screamed at me from ten feet away.

"DO NOT INTERFERE. I'M TALKING TO HIM, NOT YOU. HE'S THE MEDICAL ASSISTANT, NOT YOU."

I stared at him.

Woah. Why the fuck are we screaming?

Six words. I had managed approximately six words before this grown man decided the appropriate response was a public demonstration of his inability to regulate his emotions.

And for what? Because a woman had spoken without being invited?

Sir, have some fucking dignity.

I almost laughed. Not out of amusement, exactly, but sheer disbelief. There is something uniquely pathetic about a grown man turning scarlet with rage because a woman has attempted to finish a sentence.

I had rather naively assumed that by 2026, this particular strain of misogyny would have acquired some degree of social stigma. Not disappeared, of course. I'm not that optimistic. But perhaps become sufficiently embarrassing that men would at least have the good sense to disguise it.

Apparently, even the pretense of civility was too ambitious an expectation.

◆

For context, I work at an endocrinology practice. One of our patients had developed a habit of coming into the office for assistance applying his Dexcom continuous glucose monitor.

To be clear, there is nothing shameful about needing assistance with medical equipment. Diabetes management can be complicated, and helping patients navigate it is part of our responsibility. What is not our responsibility is functioning as a personal, on-demand Dexcom installation service whenever a patient finds himself inconvenienced.

We had explained this to him repeatedly. We could not continue applying his sensors indefinitely. If he was genuinely unable to manage them independently, we could discuss an alternative with the physician.

A perfectly reasonable distinction, I would have thought.

We also had other patients waiting, prescriptions to process, prior authorizations to complete, and an entire medical practice whose operations did not revolve around one man's glucose sensor.

He evidently found this arrangement unacceptable.

When he returned yet again, my male coworker began explaining the situation. I stepped in to reiterate what we had already told him.

"Sir, we've told you multiple—"

And there it was.

The crimson face. The bulging veins. The theatrical indignation of a man who had apparently mistaken a medical office for his personal kingdom and a female employee for an insubordinate servant.

Six words, and he'd already lost his mind. How extraordinary to possess so little self-control and yet feel so entitled to exercise authority over someone else.

What offended me wasn't that he questioned my qualifications. I know my job. My competence does not require the approval of a man who cannot manage his own temper. It was the entitlement. The extraordinary confidence with which he interrupted me, raised his voice, and ordered me to be silent in my own workplace, as though my right to speak were a privilege he could revoke.

And for what?

Because I wasn't smiling? Because I failed to perform the agreeable, deferential femininity he apparently considered a prerequisite for being heard? Because I had the audacity to tell him no without apologizing for the inconvenience of reality?

My apologies. I must have forgotten to curtsy.

So I walked away. I had work to do, and watching a grown man experience a psychological crisis over a glucose sensor seemed an exceptionally poor use of my time.

My male coworker then explained that I had worked there for years. That I was, in fact, a medical assistant. That explaining these matters was quite literally my job.

And suddenly, silence. No shouting. No indignation. No further objections.

Apparently, possession of a penis confers the extraordinary authority to determine whether another living, breathing human being is entitled to be heard.

◆

Women have spent centuries being characterized as hysterical, irrational creatures governed by emotion rather than reason. Yet here I stood, calmly explaining an office policy while a grown man turned crimson because someone had told him no.

But women are the emotional ones.

Naturally.

A woman who speaks firmly has an attitude. A woman who refuses to smile is unpleasant. A woman who declines to tolerate disrespect is unprofessional.

A man, meanwhile, can scream at an employee who hasn't finished her sentence, and his behavior is generously reclassified as frustration.

How fascinating that male anger is so often treated as a legitimate grievance, while female composure is still insufficient evidence of professionalism.

At what age does this become embarrassing?

I might excuse a toddler for throwing a tantrum when denied something he wants. His prefrontal cortex is still developing. What's this man's excuse?

Sir, have some fucking dignity.

The most revealing part was that he hadn't challenged a single thing I said. He hadn't identified an error, disputed my reasoning, or even listened long enough to understand what I was explaining.

Which raises a question I find considerably more interesting than one man's embarrassing behavior:

How much of what medicine calls patient noncompliance is, in some cases, misogyny?

How often does a female physician explain a treatment plan, only for her recommendation to become suddenly credible when a male colleague repeats it?

Medicine devotes considerable attention to barriers to treatment adherence: cost, access, health literacy, adverse effects.

As it should.

But what about the patient whose difficulty isn't comprehension, but contempt? The patient who understands the recommendation perfectly well and simply cannot tolerate receiving it from a woman? And why is the burden of resolving that prejudice so often placed on the woman herself?

Be more patient. More empathetic. More accommodating. Soften your tone. Smile. Explain it again. Don't take it personally.

How remarkably convenient that male disrespect becomes a problem for women to solve.

Patients have every right to question medical advice. Skepticism is not misogyny. But there is a difference between questioning someone's clinical judgment and deciding, before they've even finished speaking, that they have none worth considering.

One is critical thinking. The other is prejudice with an inflated sense of its own intelligence.

And in medicine, that distinction matters.

My encounter concerned a glucose-monitoring sensor. A remarkably pathetic occasion for a grown man's crisis of masculinity. But what happens when it's a medication adjustment? An abnormal laboratory result? A potentially lifesaving intervention?

How many female clinicians have had to summon a male colleague, not because they lacked the expertise, but because a patient lacked the intellectual maturity to recognize it? How much of what we call a failure of patient education is actually a patient's refusal to be educated by a woman?

Perhaps we ought to stop assuming that every communication failure is the responsibility of the person communicating. Sometimes the information is perfectly clear. The listener is simply too arrogant to receive it.

Imagine questioning a woman's competence while publicly exhibiting such an astonishing deficiency in your own.

And imagine being so spectacularly unaware of the irony.

How profoundly fucking embarrassing.
